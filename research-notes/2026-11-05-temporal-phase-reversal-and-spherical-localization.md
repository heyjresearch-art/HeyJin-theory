# Temporal Phase Reversal and the Localized Structure of Matter

**HeyJin Theory / Phase Unfolding Theory — Research Note**  
**Date:** 2026-11-05  
**Status:** Working geometric and physical hypothesis. This note records an exploratory line of reasoning; it is not an experimentally established model of photons or particles.

## Central statement

> **Time progresses in one direction, while the internal temporal-phase orientation of matter periodically reverses. The repeated reversals may allow the spatially unfolded structure of matter to remain within a bounded region, even though the complete time–phase path never moves backward in time.**

This is the key distinction between **one-way temporal progression** and **internally reversing phase orientation**. It also distinguishes persistence of a spatially localized structure from the motion of that structure's center through space.

## 1. The wave already contains time and space together

Consider the conventional traveling-wave representation

$$
\psi(x,t)=a\cos(kx-\omega t).
$$

The spatial term $kx$ and temporal term $\omega t$ are parts of the *same phase relation*. A snapshot at fixed $t$ gives a spatial wave pattern; a trace at fixed $x$ gives temporal oscillation. Neither view alone displays the full time–space relation.

A conceptual time–spatial-state path can be written as

$$
\Gamma(t)=\big(ct,\mathbf{s}(t)\big),
$$

where $\mathbf{s}(t)$ represents spatial phase-state components, not automatically a particle trajectory. Even if

$$
\mathbf{s}(t+T)=\mathbf{s}(t),
$$

the full path has advanced in time:

$$
\Gamma(t+T)\neq\Gamma(t)\qquad(T>0).
$$

**A spatial state can recur without the time–space state returning to its past.**

## 2. Three-dimensional phase structure and spherical spatial appearance

The familiar infinity-shaped (∞) path is treated as a schematic view or section of an underlying **three-dimensional phase structure**, not a literal planar track followed by a point particle.

The working geometric picture is:

- The fundamental phase structure has three-dimensional organization.
- Spatially observed components may form sphere-based oscillations or distributions.
- The distribution may exhibit relaxation/expansion and contraction over the continuously progressing temporal coordinate.
- A localized matter structure may retain its characteristic spatial range even as its internal phase state evolves.

For a spherical candidate distribution with a characteristic radius $r_s(t)$, its geometric measures are

$$
a_s(t)=4\pi r_s(t)^2,\qquad
v_s(t)=\frac{4\pi}{3}r_s(t)^3.
$$

These are *candidate distribution measures*, not a demonstrated oscillating hard surface of an elementary particle. In conventional electromagnetism, an outward spherical wavefront propagates outward while electromagnetic fields oscillate; the conjectured internal phase-sphere oscillation is not to be equated with physical retraction of that wavefront.

## 3. Light and matter: common spatial wave, different temporal-phase organization

**Light (working interpretation):** continuous open phase unfolding; its observable spatial wave pattern propagates rather than remaining localized at the emitting region.

**Matter (working interpretation):** open phase progression is repeatedly connected through closed transitions during which the *internal temporal-phase orientation* changes sign. This may permit spatial states to recur in a bounded region.

The relevant distinction is **not** that light has oscillation and matter does not. Both are described by wave/phase relations. The proposed distinction is how the internal phase progression is organized along the time direction.

Importantly, "internal reversal" **does not mean reversal of universal time, negative elapsed clock time, or travel into the past**. The common temporal coordinate remains monotonic.

## 4. Open–closed phase sequence and the reversal gap

The working schematic assigns phase-coordinate spans of $\pi$ to the open and closed sectors:

$$
\mathrm{Open}(\pi)
\rightarrow \mathrm{Closed}(\pi)
\rightarrow \mathrm{Open}(\pi)
\rightarrow \mathrm{Closed}(\pi).
$$

Over this schematic $4\pi$ span, there are $2\pi$ of open and $2\pi$ of closed phase coordinate. **This geometric 1:1 ratio is not a measured 1:1 duration ratio.**

The proposed temporal-phase-orientation sequence is

$$
\mathrm{Open}(+)
\rightarrow \mathrm{Closed}(t_c)
\rightarrow \mathrm{Open}(-)
\rightarrow \mathrm{Closed}(t_c)
\rightarrow \mathrm{Open}(+).
$$

The mathematical half-turn identity

$$
e^{i(\theta+\pi)}=-e^{i\theta}
$$

shows that a circular phase vector changes sign after $\pi$. It does **not by itself** show that a wave changes propagation direction or becomes spatially confined. The additional proposed mechanism is that the change in *internal temporal-phase orientation* is coupled to the spatial unfolding of the complete three-dimensional structure.

The term **closed transition gap** means an interval for which the normal *observable unfolding* is absent or suppressed in the model. It does not mean that the underlying phase structure disappears.

## 5. Minimal time-accounting model

For each open interval of phase span $\pi$, with open angular frequency $\omega_o$,

$$
t_o=\frac{\pi}{\omega_o}.
$$

For each closed transition, introduce a fixed candidate time-equivalent gap $t_c$ **without assigning the closed segment an independent angular frequency**.

The accounting interval for one open–closed pair is

$$
T_{\mathrm{ref}}=t_o+t_c=\frac{\pi}{\omega_o}+t_c.
$$

Its closed-gap fraction is

$$
\boxed{
r_c=\frac{t_c}{t_c+\pi/\omega_o}
=\frac{\omega_o t_c}{\pi+\omega_o t_c}.
}
$$

Within this model, if $t_c$ is fixed and $\omega_o$ increases, then $t_o$ decreases and $r_c$ increases. The number of open–closed repetitions in a fixed reference interval can also increase.

This relation is an **internal bookkeeping identity under the model definitions**, not a measured relativistic or gravitational time-dilation law. Because the closed interval is defined as not directly unfolded into observable time, $t_c$ must be understood as an **effective or inferred time-equivalent parameter**, not as a directly observed missing clock segment.

A proposed *common* $t_c$ for different particle species remains a hypothesis. It cannot be inferred from the phase division $\pi:\pi$.

## 6. Candidate role of the reversal in spatial localization

The model's qualitative reasoning is:

1. Time progresses monotonically.
2. An open phase interval produces spatially observable oscillation/unfolding.
3. At a closed transition, the internal temporal-phase orientation reverses.
4. A subsequent open interval presents a reversed internal orientation.
5. If the three-dimensional dynamics maps these reversals to recurrent spatial states, the distribution may remain localized while the entire time–space state continues to advance.

This is a **candidate mechanism**, not a proven confinement theorem. Oscillation of a field component, a $\pi$ phase sign change, and spatial localization are distinct mathematical properties. A physical wave equation, coupling relation, or field dynamics would be required to establish the proposed implication.

The movement of a material structure's center can be distinguished conceptually from its internal phase changes:

$$
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{X}(t)+\boldsymbol{\xi}(t),
$$

where $\mathbf{X}(t)$ tracks the center and $\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)$ represents internal structure. This separation is descriptive and does not presuppose a classical point-particle interior.

## 7. Numerical candidate and why it remains conditional

An earlier exploratory estimate for the electron produced

$$
t_{c,e}\simeq 7.09\times10^{-66}\ \mathrm{s}.
$$

It arose by **identifying** the weak-field gravitational clock-rate deficit at the electron's reduced Compton length,

$$
r=\frac{\hbar}{m_e c},\qquad
r_g\approx\frac{Gm_e}{rc^2}=\frac{Gm_e^2}{\hbar c},
$$

with a model closed-gap fraction and substituting the electron Compton angular frequency into the open interval. In the small-gap limit this gives

$$
t_{c,e}\approx\frac{\pi Gm_e}{c^3}.
$$

This is a conditional *inversion of assumptions*, not a measurement of an electron's internal gap. The selected reduced Compton length is not an established physical surface of the electron. The estimated gap is also much shorter than the Planck time; its literal physical interpretation is not established.

Crucially, this particular formula scales as $m$. Applying it to a muon yields a different $t_c$, whereas the proposed **universal $t_c$** would be independent of particle species. These cannot both serve as universal definitions without a revised physical connection. The numerical match for the electron alone was constructed from the same gravitational ratio and is not independent verification.
